# Decisions

A short log of non-obvious choices, thresholds and trade-offs. Newest first.

| Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-28 | **Scoring weights (owner's choice, "Balanced"):** need 30, volume 20, commercial 20, reach 20, evidence 10. Need: own words 30, repurposing 22, captions 15, consistency 12. Volume: 20 at 8 long items in 60 days, a share below. Commercial: Sponsorship 20; Service, Course, Product 16; Membership 12; Affiliate 8. Reach: email 12, form or pitch page 4, 2+ kinds of channel 4. Evidence: 10, or 6 when the country rests only on the website's domain. | Need is the largest part because IncuBrix sells a fix for it; the brief says own words score higher than an observed gap, and long episodes with no clips are IncuBrix's core job. Volume: more content means more editing and repurposing work, and weekly long-form is the brief's activity bar. Sponsorship shows a creator already earning from content; selling their own offer is next. Reach: an email is the channel most likely to be read, and a second kind of channel gives route a backup. A country domain is weaker evidence than a stated location. Two alternatives (need-first, reach-first) were offered; the owner chose Balanced. |
| 2026-10-28 | Priority: top 25 by score are A; of the rest, score 50+ is B and below 50 is C. Ties go to dedupe's order. | Owner's choice. A fixed line keeps B's meaning stable as volume grows (a share would move with the mix). |
| 2026-10-28 | Caps now trims by score (lowest first), using the same function as the score stage. | Closes the open question from caps: the recency ranking was close to arbitrary because nearly every lead posted in the last 2 days. The score depends only on the lead's own evidence, so it can be computed before stage 6 without changing the brief's stage order. |
| 2026-10-28 | `rejected.csv` is written by `engine/export.py` now. Its columns are the engine's own. | The brief lists rejected.csv in Phase 2, while its workflow gives it to the export stage; writing it from the export file keeps one stage per file. The workbook has no rejects tab, so the columns are chosen to find the creator again and check the reason. |
| 2026-10-28 | Three evidence rules from the 20-row check: "brought to you by" + the show's own name or website is not sponsorship; commercial links count only from the creator's own description or website; hosting platforms' own social accounts are not contacts. | 3 of 12 sampled qualified rows rested on this wrong evidence. Each rule is tested. The final set went from 45 to 42 leads. |
| 2026-10-28 | **Caps trims, then checks.** A country above 60% has its lowest-ranked qualified leads rejected until it is at 60%; the other rules are then checked. A rule that still fails (fewer than 3 countries, under 80% approved) does not stop the run: later stages run so the output can be inspected, but `run.py` exits with code 2 (`RUN FAILED`). | Owner's choice (asked 2026-10-28). Trimming keeps every run submittable when the mix is the only problem; today US was 33 of 51, so 6 US leads were trimmed to 27 of 45. Too few countries cannot be fixed by trimming, so it is reported, not hidden. Trimming is skipped with fewer than 3 countries, where it would discard most leads and still fail. |
| 2026-10-28 | Caps ranks leads for trimming like dedupe (most recent content, then most contacts, then ID), because the score is computed after caps (brief section 6). | Measured: almost every lead published in the last 2 days, so the ranking mostly falls to contacts and ID, close to arbitrary. **Open:** once score exists (Phase 3), trimming by score would keep the best leads. |
| 2026-10-28 | Dedupe matches qualified rows by platform ID, website domain or email, chained (union-find), and keeps the row with the most recent content, then the most contacts, then the lowest ID. | The brief names these three keys. Chaining catches a podcast and a YouTube channel linked only through a third listing. The kept row has the freshest item to cite and the most channels for route; the ID tie-break makes reruns identical. Losing rows stay in the table as Rejected with the matching key. |
| 2026-10-28 | Hosting, link-page, page-builder and network domains are never dedupe keys (`dedupe.shared_domains` plus the existing lists). | Treating every row as qualified, the first stress check merged different creators through poddyhost.com, prx.org, zencastr.com, podpage.com and others. After adding them, the remaining merges are shows of one organisation (talkSPORT, Cleveland Clinic, Hacker Noon). |
| 2026-10-28 | A qualified row is rejected when its email is the contact of 3 or more different shows across all verified rows. | Found while testing dedupe: re-upload feeds copy a famous creator's name and description, so verify and the LLM see a real creator, but the contact is the uploader's (one address on 19 such feeds, others on 56 and 9). Dr. John Campbell's qualified row was one of them. Networks (Locked On, Entercom, Bleav) share addresses the same way. 2 shows sharing an email is usually one creator (Darren Marlar's Retro Radio and Weird Darkness) and is merged instead. A creator with 3+ shows on one address would be lost; accepted as the safe side. |
| 2026-10-28 | Podcast size limit set to **250,000** linked-YouTube subscribers (was 500,000). | Owner's choice after seeing the measured sizes: 250,000 is the most common upper band in the owner's YouTube seeds (15 of 32 banded seeds). Of the 54 qualified rows, 31 of 33 with a linked channel had under 30,000 subscribers; the change drops The Human Upgrade (328,000) and TestGuild (451,000). |
| 2026-10-28 | A show whose website or contact email is on a broadcaster's or podcast network's domain (`verify.media_domains`) is rejected as an organisation. The LLM prompt also names radio/TV stations, broadcasters, media companies, networks, churches and ministries as organisations even with a named presenter. | Hand check of the 71 rows from the 2-category run: 4 were broadcaster or ministry programmes (LBC, WTMJ Radio, Vox Media, Messenger International). The domain list was built from the most common website domains in the run; hosting platforms are left off on purpose. |
| 2026-10-28 | A country quote that is a sentence or an address is put to the LLM, which says whether it is about the host (or the host's own business). Only `host` passes. | 5 of the 71 had a country quote about someone else (a staff coach, a co-founder, a company HQ, a photographer), and 1 about the topic ("Moscow is a city located in northern Idaho"). The quote is the engine's own, so the model only judges it and cannot invent evidence. A first prompt counted the host's own business address as "someone else" and wrongly dropped 13 rows; the prompt now names the host's own business, practice or show as `host`. |
| 2026-10-28 | Podcast size is read from the creator's own linked YouTube channel; above 500,000 subscribers is rejected. No link means size is not judged. | Podcasts have no public audience count, so the size band was never applied to them, and 3 celebrity-sized shows qualified. The linked channel is the only public number. 500,000 is the widest band in the owner's YouTube seeds; **pending owner review**. |
| 2026-10-28 | **Volume step:** add Podcast Index category lists (`podcast_category` seeds, trending + recently updated, 1,000 per call) as the main podcast source. | Measured: keyword search returns at most 60 shows whatever limit is asked (0 of 29 terms reached 100), and two owner terms return none. One category call returned 997-1,000 English shows, all with an episode in the last 60 days. Owner chose to do volume before dedupe. |
| 2026-10-28 | 16 category seeds added (S043-S058): Fitness, Nutrition, Mental, Self-Improvement, Entrepreneurship, Marketing, Investing, Careers, Courses, How-To, Learning, Alternative, Parenting, Relationships, Food, Design. The owner's 42 seeds are unchanged. | Categories where individual hosts (coaches, educators, practitioners) are common, since the hand check showed business and marketing shows are mostly companies. Names checked against Podcast Index's 112-category list ("Coaching" and "Language-Learning" do not exist). Pending owner review. |
| 2026-10-28 | Podcasts whose newest episode is known to be older than 60 days are dropped in discover. | They can never pass "active"; 57-61% of cached search results were inactive, and fetching their feeds was most of enrich's time. Unknown dates are kept for verify. |
| 2026-10-28 | Feeds and websites are fetched 8 at a time (`enrich.workers`); YouTube stays sequential. | The first full run took about 5 s per new show, one at a time. Different hosts can be fetched together without loading any one site; robots.txt is still checked per site. Python's thread pool, so no new dependency. |
| 2026-10-28 | Feed language tags (`en-gb`, `en-au`) are **not** used as country evidence. | Measured: only 3 of 90 active shows without a country had a non-US English tag, and `en-us` is a default many hosts use anyway. |
| 2026-10-28 | **Creator vs organisation is decided by Gemini**, only for rows that pass every other check. The model must return a verbatim quote from the supplied text (description and website); code rejects the answer if the quote is not found, and "unclear" is a rejection. | Rules could not separate company, agency and publication podcasts from creator-led businesses (5 of 9 qualifiers in the hand check). The brief allows one LLM for classification when it quotes supplied text. Owner chose this option. |
| 2026-10-28 | **Model: `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` on the free tier** (`llm.model` in `config.yaml`). **Model quality affects engine quality:** a stronger model (Flash, Pro) would make fewer wrong creator/organisation calls and write better messages in Phase 4, but costs more or is rate-limited. | Live test on 2026-10-28 with the owner's free key: `gemini-2.5-flash` is retired for new users (404); `gemini-3.8-flash` (Google's recommendation) timed out twice at 60 s and 150 s; `gemini-3.5-flash` returned 503 (high demand); `gemini-flash-latest` timed out at 90 s; `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` answered in 2.2 s. Flash-Lite is the smallest tier, so its answers are checked (verbatim quote) and hand-audited. Switching model is a one-line config change. Recorded so the live review can see the trade-off. |
| 2026-10-28 | Verify is rules-only for now. Patterns live in `engine/verify.py` with comments; thresholds live in `config.yaml` (`verify`). | Every pass carries a verbatim quote, so it can be traced and explained line by line in the live review. Patterns in YAML would be harder to read and test. |
| 2026-10-28 | Facts about the creator (country, emails, social profiles) come only from the creator's own texts: description and own website, never show notes. | Hand check: show notes describe guests and sponsors. "An Austin-based company" was a guest's, and a guest's email was accepted. |
| 2026-10-28 | When several places appear in a location phrase, the right-most wins. | "Birmingham, Alabama" was read as GB. In "City, Region" the region is the more telling part. |
| 2026-10-28 | Street addresses count only on contact or about pages or in the description; phone prefixes count on any of the creator's own pages. | A homepage listed an event venue's address. |
| 2026-10-28 | A country-code website domain (`.uk`, `.ca`, `.ie`, `.au`, `.nz`, `.sg`) is accepted as the last-resort country evidence, labelled "website domain" in the evidence. | Most of these registries require a local connection. It is weaker than a stated location, so it is used only when nothing stronger exists, and the label lets an auditor see that. |
| 2026-10-28 | Social profiles never satisfy "reachable" on their own. Only email, a contact page or a guest-pitch page can. | "Active in the last 60 days" cannot be checked for Instagram, LinkedIn or X without reading those platforms, which their terms forbid. Social links remain backup channels. |
| 2026-10-28 | The YouTube "website" is a channel-description link whose domain contains the creator's name or handle, or blank. | The first link was often a sponsor's (a bank), which made the sponsor's address look like the creator's own-domain email. |
| 2026-10-28 | Podcast feed cache bumped to `rss.v3`: show description now combines `<itunes:summary>` and `<description>`, and `<podcast:location>` is read (from the raw XML, since feedparser drops the tag text). | 26% of podcast descriptions were empty, losing the text where location and offers are stated. |
| 2026-10-28 | Organisation exclusion matches an organisation's own title or first-person self-description, not topic words. | "Smart Agency Masterclass" and "Network Marketing" are creators' topics. Wider rules would drop real expert-led creators. Company podcasts that slip through need the LLM decision (PHASES.md). |
| 2026-10-28 | Failed feed, website and robots.txt fetches are remembered for 1 day (`cache.failure_max_age_days`); failed API calls are not. | Measured: without it, a full-cache rerun still made 18 live calls (all retries of earlier timeouts) and took 9.6 min instead of seconds, breaking the "no repeat calls" rule. A day later the site gets a fresh chance. APIs are excluded because a remembered quota error would block calls after the quota resets. |
| 2026-10-28 | Parsed feeds are cached under a versioned name (`FEED_CACHE = "rss.v2"` in `engine/enrich.py`). Bump it whenever `parse_feed` output changes. | The cache stores parsed fields, not raw feeds. Without a version, results parsed by buggy older code (here: wrong timezone) would be reused for 7 days. |
| 2026-10-28 | Episode dates are converted with `calendar.timegm` (UTC), and an impossible date drops only that date, not the whole feed. | The first live run found both problems: `time.mktime` shifted every date by the local zone (+5:30 IST), and one feed with a year-99999 date was rejected entirely. |
| 2026-10-27 | Enrich skips the per-channel YouTube calls for channels outside the seed's subscriber band, stating a non-approved country, or with no uploads. They are marked `skipped: <reason>`. | Those channels cannot qualify, and quota is the bottleneck. Hidden subscriber counts and missing countries are not treated as reasons; verify decides those. |
| 2026-10-27 | `videos.list` is called per channel, not batched across channels. | Batching would save about 1 unit per channel, but the batches would change whenever the candidate set changes, so the cache would miss on reruns. Stable caching matters more than the small saving. |
| 2026-10-27 | Website pages and `robots.txt` cache their 4xx answers too (`ok_statuses`); APIs do not. | Without this, a missing contact page would be requested again on every run, breaking the "no repeat calls" rule. A 4xx from an API is an error worth seeing each time. |
| 2026-10-27 | If `robots.txt` cannot be read (network error or 5xx), the site is not fetched. | When the site's rules are unknown, the safe choice is not to read it (assignment: no scraping against a site's terms). |
| 2026-10-27 | Only the homepage plus the first same-site contact and about pages are read, and big platforms (`enrich.skip_domains`) are never fetched, though their links are kept. | That's enough for contact details, location and offer evidence, and it keeps runtime and site load low. Scraping Instagram, LinkedIn and similar sites breaks their terms, and an Instagram link is still a usable contact option. |
| 2026-10-27 | Item details are kept for 60 days (`enrich.recent_days`). | That's the longest window any rule uses (2 long-form items in 60 days, channel active in 60 days). |
| 2026-10-27 | A podcast transcript tag counts as "has captions". | It is the podcast equivalent of captions, and the absence of both is a need signal. |
| 2026-10-27 | LLM provider is **Google Gemini** (AI Studio key, free tier). It is called with plain `requests`, so there is no SDK dependency. | Owner chose it and supplied the key. Free tier means no unrefundable spend. Rules-first verify means the LLM is only needed for drafting. |
| 2026-10-27 | Sender line: "Logeshwaran, final year IT student at TCE" (`sender` in `config.yaml`). | Owner's own identity. The assignment forbids presenting the sender as an IncuBrix employee. |
| 2026-10-27 | Email postal address is a placeholder, `{sender_postal_address}`, for IncuBrix to fill at launch. | CAN-SPAM requires a valid physical postal address (a street address or PO box) in commercial email to US recipients. The seed test only goes to the owner's own test accounts, so the owner's home address is not needed or published. The Phase 4 checker will flag the placeholder as "fill before real sending". |
| 2026-10-27 | iTunes is searched in the US store only (`discover.itunes_stores`), not all 7 approved-country stores. | Live test on "founder interviews podcast": all 7 stores returned the same 38 shows (0 new outside US). Searching each store would add about 174 calls and 9 minutes per full run for nothing. The setting stays configurable. |
| 2026-10-27 | Discovery searches are cached for 7 days (`cache.search_max_age_days`). | A rerun within the week costs no quota (Phase 1 rule), and a weekly rerun still finds new creators. |
| 2026-10-27 | The database and logs live in the `--out` folder (`<out>/engine.db`, `<out>/logs/`), not at fixed paths in config. | Running with a different `--out`, for example IncuBrix's own test input in the live review, cannot mix with the main run. |
| 2026-10-27 | API keys are stripped from cache keys and are never logged. Network errors are logged by type only. | The database and logs can then be shared for evidence without leaking secrets. A new key also reuses old cached results. |
| 2026-10-27 | Only network errors, 429 and 5xx are retried. 4xx errors fail at once, and YouTube `quotaExceeded` stops YouTube for the rest of the run. | Retrying a bad request or an empty quota cannot succeed and only wastes time. |
| 2026-10-27 | When discover finds a creator twice, the first hit in seed-file order wins. The `candidates` table is rebuilt from the cache each run. | Deterministic and rerunnable. Cross-platform duplicates (same creator on YouTube and as a podcast) are handled by the dedupe stage. |
| 2026-10-27 | Discover keeps every result, including agencies and brands. Filtering happens in verify. | One stage, one job. Verify has the enriched data and records the rejection reason. |
| 2026-10-27 | Every test blocks the real network (autouse fixture in `tests/conftest.py`). | Tests must never spend quota or depend on live data. |
| 2026-10-27 | **Need evidence:** both an observed gap with a link (for example no captions, long episodes with no Shorts, upload gaps) and the creator's own words count. Own words score higher. | Own words alone would yield too few leads for 1,000. Observed gaps are still real, checkable public evidence. Owner delegated the choice. |
| 2026-10-27 | **Long-form** means 20 minutes or more (`activity.long_form_min_minutes`). | Neither the brief nor the assignment defines it. 20 minutes clearly excludes Shorts and clips while including typical podcast and tutorial lengths. Owner delegated the choice. |
| 2026-10-27 | **`leads.csv` holds qualified creators only.** Rejected creators go to `rejected.csv` with a reason. | The assignment says "all required workbook fields are complete" and counts "unique qualified creators (full leads.csv)". Rejected rows would break both. Owner delegated the choice. |
| 2026-10-27 | Rules come from the brief plus the assignment PDF. Where they differ, the stricter rule wins (for example our own audit is 40 leads at 90%, while IncuBrix samples 20 at 85%). | Passing the stricter bar passes both. |
| 2026-10-27 | `leads.csv` columns and allowed values live as constants in `engine/common.py`; segments and countries stay in `config.yaml`. | The columns are a fixed contract with the workbook, not a setting. Segments and countries are also used by the caps rules. |
| 2026-10-27 | The schema test reads the `.xlsx` as zipped XML with the standard library. | Avoids adding `openpyxl` just for a test (no new dependency without approval). |
| 2026-10-27 | API access is checked by a separate `check_apis.py`, using the cheapest calls (YouTube `videoCategories.list` = 1 unit; one Podcast Index search). It prints pass/fail only. | Proves the keys without spending search quota or ever printing secrets. It sits outside `engine/` because it is not a pipeline stage. |
| 2026-10-27 | Country codes are ISO (`GB`, not the brief's `UK`). | The Submission Workbook's country dropdown uses `US, CA, GB, IE, AU, NZ, SG, Other`. |
| 2026-10-27 | Segment names are the workbook's exact labels: `Podcaster/Video Podcaster`, `YouTuber/Video-First Creator`, `Expert-Led Creator`. Video podcasters belong to the podcaster segment. | Values are copied straight into `leads.csv`, so there is no translation step to get wrong. |
| 2026-10-27 | Seeds may only use `youtube` or `podcast`. The 8 LinkedIn and Instagram seeds (S025-27, S029-31, S034, S039) were moved to YouTube, and their need signals were rewritten as signals visible on YouTube. | The brief provides no LinkedIn or Instagram discovery API, and scraping them breaks their terms. LinkedIn and Instagram can still be a *contact* channel when a creator links them publicly. |
| 2026-10-27 | Size band split into numeric `size_min` and `size_max`. Blank for podcast seeds. | Podcast Index and iTunes publish no audience count. A blank band means "not enforced", rather than a guessed size. |
| 2026-10-27 | `need_signal_to_check` is a hint for what verify should look for, never evidence. | Evidence has to be a quote and a URL from the creator's own public content (brief, no invented data). |
| 2026-10-27 | The owner's original seed draft is kept as `data/seeds_original.csv`. | It shows what the owner wrote and what the engine changed, for the manual-work disclosure. |
| 2026-10-27 | Seeds have a `status` column (`active` or `paused`). | Lets the owner switch a niche off without deleting it. Discover will read only `active` rows. |
| 2026-10-27 | The brief is kept as `docs/BRIEF.pdf`, not converted to `BRIEF.md`. | The owner supplied a PDF. One source of truth avoids two copies drifting apart. |
| 2026-10-27 | `leads.csv` columns stay unlocked until the owner adds the Submission Workbook. | The columns must match the workbook exactly, and guessing them would mean redoing the export. |
| 2026-10-27 | LLM provider choice is deferred (`LLM_API_KEY` placeholder). | The owner's decision. It is needed by Phase 4 (drafting) at the latest. |
| 2026-10-27 | All libraries named in the brief were approved at once and pinned in `requirements.txt`. | Owner approval. It avoids asking again at each phase. Any library outside the brief still needs approval. |
| 2026-10-27 | Only direct dependencies are pinned, one `requirements.txt` for app and dev tools. | The brief asks for a single pinned file. Direct pins stay readable for the live review. |
| 2026-10-27 | `output/` and `*.db` are ignored by git. | They are generated each run and may hold contact data. If the final `leads.csv` must be committed for submission, add it explicitly then. |
| 2026-10-27 | Shared helpers go in `engine/common.py`; every other `engine/` file is exactly one stage. | Keeps "one stage, one file" while not repeating config and logging code in each stage. |
| 2026-10-27 | `config.yaml` is checked for required sections at start-up. | A broken config should fail before any API quota is spent. |
